📚 The law — Section 89(1) & Rule 21A ›

Section 89, Income-tax Act, 1961

Where an assessee receives salary in arrears or in advance, or receives in one financial year salary for more than twelve months, or a profit in lieu of salary, and is consequently assessed at a higher rate than would otherwise have applied, the Assessing Officer shall grant relief as prescribed. The relief is computed under Rule 21A of the Income-tax Rules, 1962.

Rule 21A(2) — arrears / advance of salary

  • Find the tax on the total income of the year of receipt, including the arrears — and again excluding the arrears. The difference is the tax on the arrears in the year of receipt.
  • For each relevant earlier year, find the tax on that year's total income including the portion of arrears relating to it, and excluding it. The aggregate of these differences is the tax on the arrears had they been taxed year-wise.
  • Relief = (tax on arrears in year of receipt) − (tax on arrears if taxed year-wise). If the second figure is higher, no relief is available.

Form 10E — mandatory

Relief under Section 89(1) is allowed only if Form 10E is filed electronically on the income-tax portal before filing the return. Omitting it commonly leads to the relief being disallowed and an intimation raised. Details of the arrears go in Annexure I, Table A.

Other limbs of Rule 21A cover gratuity (21A(3)/(4)), compensation on termination (21A(5)) and commuted pension (21A(6)); this tool computes the arrears/advance-salary limb.
