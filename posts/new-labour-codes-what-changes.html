India's new Labour Codes: four changes worth planning for

The Labour Codes rework the Indian workplace around a balance between worker welfare and business flexibility. Four changes carry most of the practical consequence.

A unified wage definition

A single definition of wages caps allowances at 50% of total remuneration. Pay structures built to keep basic pay low — and with it provident fund, gratuity and bonus liability — stop working as intended. Gender-based pay discrimination is prohibited outright.

Social security reaches gig and platform workers

For the first time, social security benefits extend to gig and platform workers. For businesses engaging them at scale, this is a cost line that did not previously exist.

Gratuity after one year for fixed-term employees

Fixed-term employees become eligible for gratuity after one year of service rather than five. The provisioning consequence for employers who rely on fixed-term hiring is immediate.

More flexible working arrangements

Women may work night shifts, subject to consent and safety measures, and the Codes allow for four-day working week arrangements.

The question for finance teams. The wage definition is the one that moves numbers. Before anything else, model what capping allowances at 50% does to provident fund, gratuity and bonus provisioning across your existing salary structures.
