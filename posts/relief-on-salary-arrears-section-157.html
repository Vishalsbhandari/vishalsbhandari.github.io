Relief on salary arrears: the claim people forget to make

Two lakh of arrears lands in one year, and the taxman takes a bigger cut than he should. There is a legal way to get the excess back \u2014 and a procedural step that sinks most claims before they are read.

You earned that money across several past years. It all lands in one. That single-year spike pushes you into a higher slab, so you pay more tax than you ever should have.

The law has always had an answer. Most people never claim it.

How the relief works

The mechanism is a comparison, in three steps:

  1. Recompute the tax as if each slice of arrears had been taxed in the year it was actually earned.
  2. Compare that with the tax on the lump sum in the year of receipt.
  3. Claim the difference as relief — a straight deduction from this year's tax.

Which provision applies to you

The Income-tax Act, 2025 replaced the 1961 Act with effect from 1 April 2026, and renumbered the relief, the rule and the form. Both sets of references remain in use, depending on the year in question.

 Under the 1961 ActUnder the 2025 Act
Relief provisionSection 89(1)Section 157(1)
Computation ruleRule 21ARule 73
Claim formForm 10EForm 39

Check which year you are filing for. The arithmetic is unchanged — the comparison works exactly as it always has. What changed is the paperwork. Filing the old form for a year governed by the new framework is the kind of error that gets a valid claim disallowed on a technicality.

The trap most people miss

The form must be filed on the income-tax portal before the return. Skip it and the department routinely disallows the relief and raises an intimation — even where the claim was perfectly valid on its merits.

This is the single most common reason a good section 157(1) claim fails. Not the computation. The sequence.

Who this is for

  • Anyone who received 7th CPC or DA arrears
  • A delayed increment paid out in a later year
  • Revised pension arrears
  • Advance salary received in one year for services of another
